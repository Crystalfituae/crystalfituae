<div class="pinfo__text"><p>For <a href="https://www.instagram.com/roasterscoffee_dxb/" target="_blank" rel="noopener nofollow">Roasters</a>: conversion of a 180 sqm retail shell &amp; core unit into a <strong>full cafe with kitchen, bar and seating in Sobha Hartland</strong>, Dubai, serving the families and residents of the surrounding towers.<br><br><strong>Our scope of works:</strong> interior design, technical and MEP drawings, change-of-use approvals with <strong>Sobha</strong> and the authorities, MEP and HVAC including fresh air and make-up air for the kitchen, a new steel staircase, a bespoke stainless steel bar, metal-finish joinery panels, finishing works and project management – <strong>turnkey</strong>, from an empty retail unit to an operating cafe.<br><br><strong>The challenge was turning a retail unit into a kitchen with only 34 kW of power.</strong> The unit was never planned for Food &amp; Beverage (F&amp;B): changing its use required extensive approvals with the master developer, new fresh-air and make-up air provisions and customised kitchen MEP. With 34 kW available, every piece of equipment was selected and engineered for efficiency, and the load schedule was agreed with the client before design, so the cafe runs a full menu within the limit.<br><br>A fragile existing staircase was replaced with a new granite-finished steel structure, fabricated off site and installed in one short phase. The power and ventilation limits were verified on site before works began, so the design worked within the building’s capacity rather than against it. The bar in bespoke stainless steel and metal-finish joinery suit heavy daily use. The project shows how a unit never planned for a kitchen becomes a working cafe when the engineering is solved first.</p></div>
