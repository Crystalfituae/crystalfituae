<div class="pinfo__text"><p>For <a href="https://www.instagram.com/roasterscoffee_dxb/" target="_blank" rel="noopener nofollow">Roasters</a>: conversion of a 180 sqm <strong>retail shell &amp; core unit into a full cafe</strong> with kitchen, bar and seating in Sobha Hartland, Dubai. The community is home to families and residents of the surrounding towers, who now have a specialty coffee house within walking distance.<br><br>Changing the use from retail to <strong>Food &amp; Beverage (F&amp;B)</strong> required extensive approvals with the master developer, together with <strong>fresh-air and make-up air</strong> provisions and customised MEP for the kitchen. With only <strong>34 kW of power available</strong>, every piece of equipment was carefully selected and engineered for efficiency, so the cafe could run a full menu within the limit.<br><br>A fragile existing staircase was replaced with a new <strong>granite-finished steel structure</strong>, safe and in line with the new interior. The bar was made in bespoke stainless steel, and metal-finish joinery panels give the cafe a clean, durable look that suits heavy daily use.<br><br>Crystal Fit delivered the cafe <strong>turnkey</strong> – design, technical drawings, approvals with Sobha, MEP and HVAC, steel works, bespoke joinery and finishing – from an empty retail unit to an operating cafe. The project shows how a unit that was never planned for a kitchen can become a fully working cafe when the engineering is solved first.<br><br>Before works began, the power and ventilation limits were verified on site and the equipment list was agreed with the client, so the design worked within the building’s capacity rather than against it. The new staircase was fabricated off site and installed in one short operation, which kept the rest of the works on schedule.</p></div>
